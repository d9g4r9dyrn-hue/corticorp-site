A staff, not a script: three weeks of the AI organization at work

Since the last update, the virtual team that writes, checks and improves Corticorp News and Corticorp Finance got a public page of its own, and the work it shipped in those three weeks is the best argument for it: about 175 changes to the two sites, each one filed, built and reviewed by the team.

The Editor took charge of the writing desks, the Market Pulse tools became explorers, a share button reached every page that has something worth sharing, and News stories got fuller. The Open Page Document learned to read Word files, equations and review comments. And today corticorp.com relaunched around the idea underneath all of it.

The team

The organization, in public

On October 30 the team page went up: one human operator, a Board of five roles that reads the code and never the live data, and a Newsroom that reads the data and never the code, with the market desks, news desks and simulated fund managers beneath them. It shows how a day's editions get written, how the team files work on itself, and the rules that took the longest to get right: separation of powers, accountability in public, and a chain of command where the merge, the deploy and the production keys are out of every AI role's reach.

Two changes to the team itself came on October 25. The Editor became chief of the writing desks, directing them by written note and holding the house rules on voice, facts and named people. And the daily jobs moved to the newest generation of Claude models.

Finance

Tools you can walk through, and a story before the chart

The four Market Pulse desk tools were rebuilt as explorers between October 28 and 29. Market Anatomy opens on the day's standout name, the Commodity Explorer and Sector Supply Lines follow the relationships between markets (gas, crude and grains paths were added on October 31), and the Macro board became a single poster with a related-figure explorer around it. Each tool names its day, links across to the others, and can be shared.

The share cards changed how they are made. Since November 4 the line that goes with a card is written from the day's story first, and the picture is chosen to support it, not the other way round. Since November 9 those lines also vary in form, so a week of posts does not read as one sentence repeated. Composite charts gained a proper legend.

Behind the scenes the data underneath grew a great deal. Finance now stores, with history, a physical-world batch (port traffic, river levels, drought, hurricanes and quakes), market-structure feeds (Treasury auctions, repo rates, positioning), precious-metals holdings and trade, and its own seven-input sentiment composite, which replaced a third-party fear and greed index. And the Pulse no longer leads with a move it cannot explain.

Simulated portfolios. A research experiment, not investment advice.

Elsewhere

What changed on everything else

Corticorp News: fuller stories, the whole archive, and quicker taps

A Highlights tab collects every story tagged by feel, with one filter for each. Taps answer at once, and pages weigh about a third less. Since November 9 the time-range picker searches the full history instead of only the newest stories, and since November 10 a story that arrived as a single thin paragraph is rewritten from its source article, keeping every name the original carried. New sources include Wikipedia's current events, Statistics Canada, the UK's ONS, Carbon Brief, Mongabay and VOA.

opd: Word files, equations and review comments

In the week after the last update the Open Page Document moved to version 1.4 of its format, with page setup by section, numbered lists and annotations. The editor reads Word documents, handles equations and charts, takes review comments and tracked suggestions, and can redact. It downloads as Word, OpenDocument or PDF. Imports are now checked against the original with a visual yardstick. Still pre-alpha.
