Corticorp Finance

Live

The most ambitious thing I have built. Thirty-nine simulated portfolios run side by side on the same live market, each with its own stated investment discipline, each starting from the same $100,000, and each publishing every decision it makes along with the reasoning and the exact inputs it saw. It began as a tab inside Corticorp News and in late August 2026 it moved out to its own domain, its own masthead and its own five sections. Simulated, and not investment advice; the point is the experiment, not a tip.

  • Thirteen model-driven strategists plus twenty-six documented sleeve portfolios, including two deliberate control arms that exist so the roster has something honest to be measured against
  • An AI Board reviewing the experiment in public: a Judge, a Scientist, a Treasurer, an Auditor, an Editor, a research Librarian and a Marketer, with their findings and open work items published rather than kept internal
  • Delta-1 risk telemetry: every decision carries a snapshot of what a 1, 3, 5 or 10 percent market fall does to that exact book, computed by revaluing each position rather than by a rule of thumb
  • A written register of every change to the models behind it, because changing a model changes the measuring instrument and a track record that spans one is two records, not one
  • Two products from one codebase, routed by hostname, each domain publishing only its own pages, sitemap and robots file

World’s Eye View

Live

Around 34,700 public webcams from 31 open sources, quilted onto one satellite map as live thumbnails rather than pins. Zoom out and you get the notable ones; zoom in and a city fills with windows. Because every frame is real and refreshes on its own schedule, the map drifts through the day with the world, and you can watch dawn cross a continent.

  • Server-side thinning that projects every candidate into screen pixels and keeps the most interesting camera per grid cell, which is what stops 4,800 Florida cameras becoming a solid wall
  • Frames proxied and downscaled to roughly 6KB each, so one origin serves every image and the security policy never has to trust dozens of third-party hosts
  • A multicam wall, cameras along a driving route, and a finder for wherever sunrise or sunset is happening right now
  • The map needs no database at all; the one behind it exists only for optional accounts and saved walls, and the map keeps working without it

AlgoRythmic

Live

A playlist builder for YouTube Music that treats a playlist as a rule set you can edit. Describe a vibe in plain English and it compiles to visible selection rules: era, length, no live versions, never the same artist twice. If the queue is wrong, you can see which rule made it wrong, which is the thing no streaming service will let you do.

  • Built around one hard constraint: the API gives 10,000 quota units a day and a 50-track save costs about 2,550, so everything except saving runs free against a local mirror of your library
  • Seed a queue from one song and weight eight dimensions of what "similar" is allowed to mean
  • Every unit spent is written to a ledger, and a call that would blow the day's budget is refused rather than failing halfway through a save
  • Saves a real private playlist, so it plays in the car with background playback and lock-screen controls, which an embedded player cannot do

2026 · In the lab

CortComp

Prototype
Upward (softs ↑) Downward (louds ↓) Invert (beyond ∞:1)

A dynamics processor that does something most compressors physically can't: make the softs loud and the louds soft — past the point of a limiter. Its INVERT mode drives the transfer slope negative (beyond ∞:1), so louder input yields quieter output: true dynamic inversion.

  • Header-only, JUCE-free DSP engine — unit-testable offline, no framework lock-in
  • Stereo-linked feedforward detector that reacts to the louder channel to preserve the stereo image
  • Three simultaneous behaviors — downward, upward, and inversion — from one clean transfer curve
